Multiplying by i means turning 90 degrees on the plane. Two turns make 180 degrees, which is the same as multiplying by negative one. That is the whole meaning of i² = -1.
A complex number has two coordinates: horizontal for the real part and vertical for the imaginary part. Every complex number is a point on this flat plane. A real number sits strictly on the horizontal axis, and a pure imaginary number sits strictly on the vertical axis.
Adding two complex numbers means walking in two directions separately. Real parts add together horizontally, imaginary parts add together vertically. Each direction minds its own business.
The conjugate keeps the real part and flips the imaginary part. On the plane, it is a mirror reflection across the real horizontal axis. This is different from the negative, which flips both parts.
The modulus is simply how far a point is from the center — a straight-line distance. The horizontal and vertical distances form a right triangle, and the modulus is the hypotenuse.
The argument is the direction a point faces, measured as an angle from the positive horizontal axis. Anticlockwise is positive, clockwise is negative. The exact same direction can be described by different angles.
Multiplying two complex numbers means stretching the distance and turning the angle at the same time. Distances multiply, and angles add together.
The exact same point can be described two ways. Rectangular form says "go right, go up". Polar form says "face this direction, walk this far". Neither is more correct. Rectangular is easier for addition. Polar is easier for multiplication.
Multiplying by i repeatedly steps around the circle in 90-degree jumps. The cycle repeats every four steps. Any power of i can be found by seeing where you land after jumping around the circle.
To find the direction of any point, find the small angle it makes with the nearest flat horizontal axis — that is the reference angle. Then use the quadrant to figure out the actual principal direction.
