Part A · Foundations for M01

01

A function connects input and output

Imagine a number machine. Whatever number you put in, it doubles it and adds one. The answer changes when the input changes, but the machine keeps following the same rule.

Connect it: input = x; rule = f(x) = 2x + 1; output = y. One input and its output give one graph point, (x, y).

On a small screen, swipe the diagram sideways.

Predict: if the input rises by one, how much does the output rise?

Check your idea

It rises by two. Doubling the extra input adds two to the output; the fixed “add one” stays the same.

02

Adding or multiplying?

Two games start you with one credit. The first gives you one extra credit each round. The second doubles whatever you already have. They match after the first round, but then the doubling game pulls away.

Connect it: equal rounds represent equal input steps. Adding gives 1 + n; doubling gives 2n. Exponential growth repeats a factor, so its increases need not stay equal.

Doubling: 2ⁿAdding: 1 + n

Predict: does the doubling game add the same number of credits each round?

Check your idea

No. The multiplying factor stays at two, but the increases are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. A bigger starting amount produces a bigger increase.

This game uses whole rounds. The mathematical exponential function also has values between whole-number inputs.

03

Rewind the multiplying process

At the start of a film, a glowing bar has length one. Each forward step doubles its length. Rewind the film: each backward step halves it. The bar becomes smaller, but it never acquires a negative length.

Connect it: position zero has value 20 = 1. Moving backwards divides: 2−1 = ½ and 2−2 = ¼. A negative exponent means reciprocal, not a negative output.

Predict: after three backward steps from one, is the value negative, zero or positive?

Check your idea

Positive: one eighth. Dividing a positive amount by two keeps it positive. This also explains why e raised to a negative power remains positive.

The animation shows exponents from −4 to 3. The mathematics continues beyond that range. A finite display cannot demonstrate infinitely many steps.

04

A point and a slope make a line

Fix one end of a straight ramp at a marked height. You can tilt it upwards, keep it level, or tilt it downwards. Moving the whole ramp higher changes its position; changing its tilt changes how much it rises for each step across.

Connect it: slope = vertical change ÷ horizontal change. Here the marked point is (0, b). Choosing its height and the slope fixes the line.

LineOne step across, then the vertical change

Through (a, b), with slope m:   y − b = m(x − a).

Predict: if the entire ramp is lifted, does its slope change?

Check your idea

No. Both ends move up equally, so their difference in height is unchanged. The slope depends on the changes between points, not the height of either point alone.

05

An amount is different from a rate

A toy car rolls faster and faster. Watch two readings: how far it has travelled, and how fast it is moving now. The first tells you the total distance so far. The second tells you how quickly that distance is changing.

Connect it: in this simple model, d(t) = t² metres and d′(t) = 2t metres per second. Graph height is distance; tangent slope is speed.

Distance curveTangent: local rateHeight above zero

At two seconds, both readings show four. Does that make distance and speed the same thing?

Check your idea

No: 4 metres and 4 metres per second have different meanings and units. At three seconds, the readings are 9 metres and 6 metres per second. Equal numbers do not make the quantities identical.

This is an illustrative motion model over 0–3 seconds, not a measurement of a real car.

Part B · Discover why e is special

06

From two points to one local slope

Imagine measuring how steep a curved path is at one spot. A straight ruler joining two distant points gives an average steepness. Keep one point fixed and bring the other closer. The ruler settles towards the path’s local direction.

Connect it: for y = 2ˣ, hold A = (0, 1) fixed and move B = (h, 2ʰ). Their secant slope is (2ʰ − 1) / h.

CurveSecant through A and BLimiting tangent at A

When both changes become tiny, must their ratio approach zero?

Check your idea

No. Two shrinking quantities can have a nonzero ratio. Here the secant slopes approach about 0.693147. At h = 0 the quotient is 0/0 and undefined; the limiting tangent slope still exists.

The purple tangent shows the established limiting value for comparison. This finite animation illustrates the limit; it does not prove it. A tangent matches local slope and need not meet a curve only once.

07

Choose the base: find e

Several curved paths pass through the same meeting point. Notice that some head uphill more sharply than others. Adjust one path until, at that meeting point, its local slope is exactly one. That setting has a special name.

Connect it: all positive bases give a⁰ = 1, so the curves meet at (0, 1). The base called e ≈ 2.71828 gives tangent slope exactly 1 there.

Selected exponentialIts tangent at (0, 1)Reference slope 1

Predict: can a curve stay above zero while its slope is negative? Try base ½.

Check your idea

Yes. The curve for base ½ is positive but decreases as x increases. Height and slope describe different things. Base 1 gives a horizontal line; bases above 1 give increasing curves.

e is a fixed number, not a variable. “Choose e” uses the browser’s stored approximation. The exact property comes from mathematics, not from tuning a finite slider or rounding a readout to 1.

08

For eˣ, height and slope match

Follow the special curve and take two readings at each stop: its height and its local slope. Wherever you stop, the numbers match. Far to the left both are small; farther right both are larger. They still describe different features.

Connect it: for f(x) = eˣ, f′(x) = eˣ. Move the point. The vertical guide measures height; the small tangent triangle measures slope as rise ÷ run.

Curve eˣTangentHeight

Predict: at a negative input, is the height negative? Is the slope negative?

Check your idea

Neither. Both equal e raised to that negative input, which is a positive reciprocal. The curve remains above the x-axis and is increasing. It approaches y = 0 as x heads towards negative infinity, without reaching zero at any finite input.

Domain: all real inputs. Range: positive outputs. Intercept: (0, 1). Horizontal asymptote: y = 0 on the left. A tiny positive height may look like zero on a screen; the readout helps distinguish them. Section 10 connects the slope rule to the algebra.

09

Lift the curve or stretch it

Copy a curved track onto a transparent sheet. Sliding the sheet upwards lifts every point equally, leaving the slopes unchanged. Stretching the drawing vertically is different: the rises become larger over the same horizontal distances, so the slopes change too.

Connect it: compare the original eˣ with g(x) = keˣ + c. We inspect both at x = 0. Here k controls stretching; c controls lifting.

Original eˣChanged curveChanged tangent at x = 0

g′(x) = keˣ   — the constant c contributes zero.

Predict: with k = 1 and c = −3, can the height be negative while the slope stays positive?

Check your idea

Yes. At x = 0, the height is −2 and the slope is 1. Being below the axis does not mean decreasing. With c = 0, height and slope also match for keˣ; adding a nonzero c breaks that equality.

10

Connect the pictures to the algebra

The pictures give you something to think with. These short mathematical connections explain the important steps. Keep x fixed while h changes.

  1. An extra input interval adds a multiplying factor.

    ex+h = eˣeʰ

    This is the index law. For instance, 2³ × 2² = 2⁵: three factors of two and another two factors give five factors in total. The law also holds for real exponents with a positive base.

  2. Factor out what the two heights share.

    ex+h − eˣ = eˣ(eʰ − 1)

    Compare 5 × 7 − 5 = 5(7 − 1). The common factor is not lost; it multiplies the whole bracket.

  3. Divide the change in height by the interval.

    (ex+h − eˣ) / h = eˣ · (eʰ − 1) / h,   h ≠ 0

    The factor eˣ stays fixed. The remaining quotient is the same one used to investigate slope at (0, 1).

  4. Use the established limiting property of e.

    As h approaches zero, (eʰ − 1) / h approaches 1. Therefore the derivative is eˣ × 1 = eˣ.

    A table or animation suggests the behaviour; this step uses the exact established limit. It does not substitute h = 0.
